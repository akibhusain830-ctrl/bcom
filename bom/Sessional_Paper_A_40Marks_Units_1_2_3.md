## ⚡ Section A: Short Answer Questions (2 Marks × 5 = 10 Marks)

### Q1 (Unit 1): Define Scientific Management and state two of its key techniques.
* **Mnemonic Formula:** **F - T** (Functional foremanship, Time study)
* **Definition:** F.W. Taylor defined Scientific Management as: *"Knowing exactly what you want men to do and seeing that they do it in the best and cheapest way."* It replaces old rules of thumb (guesswork) with exact, researched scientific methods.
* **Two Core Techniques:**
  1. **Functional Foremanship:** Dividing factory worker supervision under 8 specialized bosses (4 for planning work, 4 for executing work).
  2. **Time Study:** Using a stopwatch to measure the exact standard time a well-trained worker needs to complete a given task.
* **Examiner Tip:** Quote F.W. Taylor and list 2 distinct techniques for full 2/2 marks.

### Q2 (Unit 1): Explain the concepts of "Perpetual Succession" and "Common Seal" in a Joint Stock Company.
* **Mnemonic Formula:** **P - S** (Permanent life, Stamp signature)
* **Answer:**
  1. **Perpetual Succession:** A company is created by law and has continuous existence independent of its members. *"Members may come and members may go, but the company goes on forever."* Death, insolvency, or exit of shareholders does not dissolve the company.
  2. **Common Seal:** Being an artificial legal person, a company cannot sign documents by hand. The common seal acts as its official signature affixed to contracts and share certificates by authorized directors.
* **Examiner Tip:** Quote: *"Members may come and go, but the company continues forever."*

### Q3 (Unit 3): What are Planning Premises? State one internal and one external premise.
* **Mnemonic Formula:** **I - E** (Inside budget vs Outside market)
* **Definition:** **Planning Premises** are the future assumptions, forecasts, and anticipated market conditions upon which business plans and strategies are formulated.
* **Two Categories of Premises:**
  1. **Internal Premises (Controllable):** Assumptions regarding factors within the enterprise, such as available capital budget, machinery capacity, and worker skill levels.
  2. **External Premises (Uncontrollable):** Assumptions regarding external market forces, such as government tax policies, inflation rates, and competitor price moves.
* **Examiner Tip:** Distinguish clearly between internal (controllable) and external (uncontrollable) assumptions.

### Q4 (Unit 2): Define Business Ethics and state two reasons why ethical conduct is essential.
* **Mnemonic Formula:** **T - R** (Trust, Reputation)
* **Definition:** **Business Ethics** is the set of moral principles, values, and behavioral rules that govern the choices, conduct, and decisions of commercial enterprises in the marketplace, directing managers to do what is right, fair, and just.
* **Why Ethical Conduct is Essential:**
  1. **Builds Long-Term Consumer Trust:** Customers remain loyal to brands that maintain honesty in pricing, product safety, and fair advertising (e.g., Tata Group).
  2. **Prevents Severe Legal Penalties:** Adhering to ethical norms prevents costly government penalties, consumer court lawsuits, and reputational collapse.
* **Corporate Example:** Tata Group adhering to ethical governance with zero bribery tolerance.
* **Examiner Tip:** Give Tata Group as a corporate example of ethical governance.

### Q5 (Unit 3): What is Management by Objectives (MBO)? State its two core pillars.
* **Mnemonic Formula:** **J - R** (Joint goal-setting, Results-based review)
* **Definition:** **Management by Objectives (MBO)**, introduced by Peter Drucker in 1954, is a management system where superiors and subordinates jointly identify common corporate targets, define each individual’s responsibilities, and evaluate performance based on actual results achieved.
* **Two Core Pillars:**
  1. **Joint Goal-Setting:** Managers and workers sit together to agree on clear, realistic, and measurable targets rather than orders being dictated top-down.
  2. **Periodic Results Review:** Employees are evaluated objectively against agreed target results rather than personal supervisor favoritism.
* **Examiner Tip:** Name Peter Drucker (1954) and emphasize "Joint Goal-Setting".

### Q6 (Unit 1): Explain five key factors governing the choice of an ideal form of business organisation.
* **Mnemonic Formula:** **C - L - C - M - F** (Capital, Liability, Continuity, Management, Flexibility)
* **Visual Blueprint:**
```text
Choice of Business Form:
├── 1. Capital Requirement (Small = Sole Prop, Huge = Joint Stock Co)
├── 2. Nature of Liability (Unlimited = Sole/Partner, Limited = Company)
├── 3. Continuity & Stability (Perpetual Company vs Temporary Sole Prop)
├── 4. Managerial Talent Needed (Simple Solo vs Professional Board)
└── 5. Secrecy & Flexibility (100% Secret Sole Prop vs Public Disclosures)
```
* **Model Answer:**
  1. **Capital Requirement:**
     - Small businesses requiring modest capital (like local bakeries or tailoring shops) operate best as Sole Proprietorships or Partnerships.
     - Large industrial ventures (like steel mills or airlines) requiring hundreds of crores choose Joint Stock Companies to raise funds from the general public.
  2. **Nature of Liability:**
     - Sole proprietors and general partners bear **unlimited liability**, meaning personal family assets can be seized to settle business debts.
     - Investors who wish to shield their personal assets choose a Company format where liability is strictly limited to unpaid share capital.
  3. **Continuity and Stability:**
     - If permanent, uninterrupted survival is essential, a Joint Stock Company is ideal because it possesses perpetual succession.
     - Sole proprietorships and partnerships are unstable, dissolving upon the death, illness, or bankruptcy of the owners.
  4. **Managerial Talent & Specialization:**
     - A single owner cannot master marketing, finance, engineering, and legal affairs simultaneously. Enterprises requiring multi-disciplinary leadership require corporate structures with professional boards.
  5. **Business Secrecy and Legal Formalities:**
     - Sole proprietorship offers 100% decision flexibility and complete business secrecy without publishing financial accounts.
     - Companies face heavy government regulation, mandatory public audits, and loss of trade privacy.
* **Examiner Tip:** Cover Capital, Liability, Continuity, Management, and Secrecy for full 5 marks.

### Q7 (Unit 1): Distinguish between Henri Fayol’s Administrative Management and F.W. Taylor’s Scientific Management with a comparison matrix.
* **Comparison Matrix:**

| Basis of Distinction | Administrative Management (Henri Fayol) | Scientific Management (F.W. Taylor) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Primary Focus** | Overall top-level administration and 14 general principles of office management. | Shop-floor worker efficiency, machine operation, and physical task speed. |
| **2. Direction of Approach** | **Top-Down:** Starts at top executives and flows downward to workers. | **Bottom-Up:** Starts at worker tools on factory machines and flows upward. |
| **3. Scope of Applicability**| Universally applicable to all types of enterprises (offices, banks, colleges). | Best suited for manufacturing plants, assembly lines, and factory workshops. |
| **4. Core Contribution** | Developed 14 Universal Principles and 5 management functions (POSDC). | Developed Scientific Principles (Rules of thumb replaced, Time/Motion study). |
| **5. Unity of Command** | Strictly insisted that a subordinate must receive orders from **only one boss**. | Rejected rigid unity of command; introduced **Functional Foremanship** with 8 bosses. |
| **6. Wage Philosophy** | Recommended fair remuneration based on living costs and company capacity. | Recommended **Differential Piece Wage System** (high pay for fast workers, low pay for slow workers). |

* **Examiner Tip:** Highlight the difference in Unity of Command (Fayol: strictly 1 boss; Taylor: 8 bosses) for full marks.

### Q8 (Unit 2): Explain Archie Carroll’s Four Pillars of Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) with a pyramid diagram.
* **Mnemonic Formula:** **E - L - E - P** (Economic, Legal, Ethical, Philanthropic)
* **Pyramid Diagram:**
```text
        ▲
       / \       4. PHILANTHROPIC (Voluntary charity, community upliftment)
      /───\\      3. ETHICAL       (Fairness, moral values, beyond law)
     /─────\\     2. LEGAL         (Obey statutes, comply with tax rules)
    /───────\\    1. ECONOMIC      (Be profitable — the essential foundation)
   /─────────\\
```
* **Model Answer:**
  1. **Economic Responsibility (The Foundation Tier — "Be Profitable"):**
     - The primary requirement of any enterprise: produce goods and services society demands, create employment, and generate reasonable profit to ensure commercial survival.
  2. **Legal Responsibility (Second Tier — "Obey the Law"):**
     - Society requires businesses to play by the legal rules of the economic game. Must obey labor laws, consumer protection acts, pollution standards, and pay statutory taxes.
  3. **Ethical Responsibility (Third Tier — "Be Ethical"):**
     - Obligation to do what is fair, right, and just, even when not explicitly mandated by written law (e.g., fair vendor payment cycles, avoiding deceptive advertisements).
  4. **Philanthropic Responsibility (The Apex Tier — "Be a Good Corporate Citizen"):**
     - Voluntary corporate charity: donating corporate funds toward public schools, rural healthcare, disaster relief, and community welfare programs.
* **Corporate Example:** Tata Group fulfilling all four tiers by maintaining profitable steel mills (Economic), complying with environmental laws (Legal), paying fair wages (Ethical), and funding cancer research hospitals (Philanthropic).
* **Examiner Tip:** Always draw the 4-tier pyramid diagram! Guarantees 5/5 marks in GU exams.

### Q9 (Unit 3): Explain the limitations of planning in a dynamic business environment and how managers can overcome them.
* **Mnemonic Formula:** **R - D - C - T - F** (Rigidity, Dynamic change, Cost, Time, False security)
* **Model Answer:**
  1. **Planning Leads to Rigidity:** Once formalized, employees follow pre-set schedules blindly. When local market realities change unexpectedly, rigid procedures prevent workers from using personal initiative.
  2. **Inaccuracy in Dynamic Markets:** Planning relies on forecasts about the future. Rapid technological shifts, sudden inflation spikes, or government policy changes can render forecasts completely wrong.
  3. **Heavy Financial Costs:** Gathering market intelligence, hiring strategy consultants, and conducting board meetings consumes massive financial expenditure.
  4. **Time-Consuming Delays:** Debating, writing, and approving elaborate plans takes weeks or months, causing companies to miss sudden lucrative market opportunities.
  5. **False Sense of Security:** Managers mistakenly believe that having a written plan guarantees business success, leading to operational complacency.
* **Measures to Overcome Limitations:**
  - **Adopt Contingency Planning:** Prepare flexible alternative courses of action (Plan B and Plan C).
  - **Use Rolling Forecasts:** Continuously update quarterly forecasts rather than clinging to rigid 5-year plans.
  - **Encourage Ground-Level Feedback:** Empower branch managers to adapt procedures to immediate local conditions.
* **Examiner Tip:** Explain the dynamic environment and provide Contingency Planning as the practical solution.

### Q10 (Unit 1): Discuss Henri Fayol’s 14 Principles of Management. Explain the top 7 principles in detail with simple real-world examples and draw the Gang Plank diagram.
* **Mnemonic Formula:** **D - A - D - U - U - S - R** (Division, Authority, Discipline, Unity of command, Unity of direction, Subordination, Remuneration)
* **Gang Plank Diagram:**
```text
FAYOL'S SCALAR CHAIN & GANG PLANK:
           A (Top Boss)
         /   \
       B       E
     /           \
   C               F
 /                   \
D ◄── GANG PLANK ───► G  (Direct lateral shortcut for emergencies between D & G)
```
* **Model Answer:**
  - **Introduction:** Henri Fayol (1841–1925), known as the "Father of Modern Operational Management," formulated 14 universal principles of management to guide executives in running organizations effectively.
  - **Top 7 Core Principles Explained with Real-World Examples:**
    1. **Division of Work (Specialization):**
       - Divide large complex tasks into smaller specialized jobs. Repeating the same task builds worker speed, accuracy, and operational efficiency.
       - *Example:* In a bank, separate clerks handle cash deposits, loan underwriting, and customer inquiries.
    2. **Authority and Responsibility:**
       - Authority is the formal right to give orders; responsibility is the obligation to perform duties. They must be evenly balanced: giving authority without responsibility breeds abuse of power, while responsibility without authority causes frustration.
       - *Example:* A sales manager given the target of Rs 10 lakh sales must be granted the authority to offer 5% customer discounts.
    3. **Discipline:**
       - Sincere obedience, respect for organizational rules, and honoring employment contracts at all levels. Requires fair supervisors and clear rules.
       - *Example:* Workers arriving on time and management paying promised performance bonuses punctually.
    4. **Unity of Command:**
       - An employee must receive orders from **only one superior**. Receiving contradictory orders from two bosses creates confusion, delays, and conflict.
       - *Example:* A software engineer reporting strictly to one Project Lead rather than being pulled between two managers.
    5. **Unity of Direction:**
       - One head and one unified plan for a group of corporate activities sharing the same objective. It prevents conflicting departmental efforts.
       - *Example:* A company producing both cars and motorcycles must have separate independent marketing divisions for each product line.
    6. **Subordination of Individual Interest to General Interest:**
       - The collective targets of the company must take absolute priority over any single worker or manager’s selfish desires.
       - *Example:* An executive refusing to purchase raw materials from a family member's firm at inflated rates.
    7. **Fair Remuneration:**
       - Workers must receive fair wages that guarantee a dignified standard of living while staying within the company’s financial capacity.
       - *Example:* Providing medical insurance and inflation-adjusted dearness allowances to retain talented staff.
  - **Scalar Chain and Gang Plank:**
    - The Scalar Chain is the formal, unbroken line of authority flowing from top to bottom. In emergency crises, Fayol introduced the **Gang Plank**—a direct lateral bridge enabling two employees of identical rank in different departments (D and G) to communicate directly, provided their immediate superiors are notified.
  - **Summary of Remaining 7 Principles:**
    - Centralization (optimum balance), Order (everything in its place), Equity (fair, unbiased treatment), Stability of Tenure (low employee turnover), Initiative (encouraging creativity), Esprit de Corps (team harmony).
* **Examiner Tip:** Draw the inverted-V Scalar Chain / Gang Plank diagram; guarantees full 10/10 marks.

### Q11 (Unit 2): Discuss the various components of the Business Environment. Explain the Micro and Macro (PESTLE) layers with a comprehensive structural diagram.
* **Mnemonic Formula:** **INTERNAL + MICRO (Customers, Suppliers, Rivals) + MACRO (P-E-S-T-L-E)**
* **Structural Diagram:**
```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        BUSINESS ENVIRONMENT                            │
├───────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┤
│      MICRO LAYER (Immediate)      │     MACRO LAYER (PESTLE Forces)    │
├───────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Customers & Buyers              │ • Political: Govt stability, laws  │
│ • Suppliers & Vendors             │ • Economic: Inflation, GDP growth  │
│ • Competitors & Rivals            │ • Socio-Cultural: Demographics     │
│ • Marketing Intermediaries        │ • Technological: Automation, AI    │
│ • Financing Bodies                │ • Legal: Acts of parliament        │
│                                   │ • Environmental: Climate standards │
└───────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────┘
```
* **Model Answer:**
  - **Introduction:** The business environment comprises the aggregate total of all individuals, external institutions, economic forces, and societal factors outside the immediate control of an enterprise that shape its performance, profitability, and survival.
  - **I. Internal Environment (Controllable Forces):**
    - Value system, organizational culture, physical assets, liquidity reserves, and workforce competencies.
  - **II. Micro Environment (Immediate Task Forces):**
    1. **Customers:** Customer preferences, income levels, and purchasing behavior dictate product survival.
    2. **Suppliers:** Furnish raw materials and machinery. Unreliable suppliers cause factory shutdowns.
    3. **Competitors:** Direct rivals fighting for market share. Pricing wars and promotions shape competition.
    4. **Marketing Intermediaries:** Wholesalers, retail chains, and logistical agencies connecting the firm to end consumers.
    5. **Financiers:** Commercial banks and shareholders providing working and fixed capital.
  - **III. Macro Environment (Broad PESTLE Forces):**
    1. **Political Environment (P):** Government stability, foreign trade policies, and political ideology.
    2. **Economic Environment (E):** National GDP growth rates, interest rates, inflation levels, and disposable income.
    3. **Socio-Cultural Environment (S):** Population demographics, literacy rates, lifestyle trends, and traditions.
    4. **Technological Environment (T):** Digital transformation, internet commerce, automated robotics, and AI.
    5. **Legal Environment (L):** Statutory legislation enacted by parliament (Companies Act 2013, Consumer Protection Act).
    6. **Environmental / Natural Environment (E):** Pollution standards, climate change risks, and green energy mandates.
* **Corporate Example:** Tata Motors adapting to technological and environmental layers by investing heavily in electric vehicles (EVs).
* **Examiner Tip:** Draw the concentric layers diagram and explain all 6 letters of PESTLE clearly.

### Q12 (Unit 3): Explain the meaning, significance, and 7-step process of Decision-Making in management with a visual flowchart.
* **Mnemonic Formula:** **I - D - A - E - S - I - F** (Identify, Diagnose, Alternatives, Evaluate, Select, Implement, Feedback)
* **Sequential Flowchart:**
```text
[1. Identify Problem] ──► [2. Diagnose Causes] ──► [3. Develop Alternatives] ──► [4. Evaluate Choices]
                                                                                       │
[7. Follow-up & Feedback] ◄── [6. Implement Choice] ◄── [5. Select Optimal Option] ◄───┘
```
* **Model Answer:**
  - **Meaning:** Decision-making is the cognitive, rational process of choosing the best course of action among competing alternatives to resolve an operational problem or achieve an organizational goal.
  - **The 7-Step Process:**
    1. **Step 1: Identify and Define the Problem:** Recognize that a discrepancy exists between actual organizational performance and desired goals (e.g., a 15% drop in product sales).
    2. **Step 2: Diagnose Root Causes:** Investigate deeper underlying causes rather than treating superficial symptoms (e.g., is low sales due to poor quality, rude staff, or competitor discounts?).
    3. **Step 3: Develop Alternative Solutions:** Brainstorm creative potential courses of action using cross-functional team discussions and market research.
    4. **Step 4: Evaluate the Alternatives:** Scrutinize every prospective option against four critical criteria: financial cost, technical feasibility, potential risks, and expected payoff.
    5. **Step 5: Select the Optimal Alternative:** Choose the alternative that maximizes return while minimizing risk. Under conditions of incomplete market data, managers pick the best realistic "satisficing" option.
    6. **Step 6: Implement the Decision:** Translate the chosen strategy into concrete action: communicate the decision, assign individual responsibilities, and allocate budgets.
    7. **Step 7: Follow-up and Feedback Loop:** Monitor actual performance results against initial projections. If the outcome deviates negatively, managers initiate corrective modifications.
* **Corporate Example:** Netflix deciding in 2007 to transition from mailing physical DVDs to online digital streaming.
* **Examiner Tip:** Draw the 7-step sequential flowchart with the feedback loop; repeats frequently in GU exams.

### Q13 (Unit 3): Explain the concept of Delegation of Authority. Discuss the Trinity of Delegation (Authority, Responsibility, Accountability). Can accountability be delegated? Discuss major barriers to delegation.
* **Mnemonic Formula:** **A - R - A** (Authority, Responsibility, Accountability) | *"Accountability is Absolute"*
* **Visual Diagram:**
```text
THE TRINITY OF DELEGATION:
1. AUTHORITY      ──► Flows Downward (Right to direct & spend)
2. RESPONSIBILITY ◄── Flows Upward   (Obligation to perform assigned task)
3. ACCOUNTABILITY ◄── Flows Upward   (Ultimate answerability to higher boss — CANNOT BE DELEGATED)
```
* **Model Answer:**
  - **Meaning:** Delegation is the downward transfer of formal decision-making authority and operational duties from a superior manager to an immediate subordinate. It empowers managers to focus on strategic leadership while subordinates handle daily operational tasks.
  - **The Trinity of Delegation (The Three Core Elements):**
    1. **Authority (The Right to Command):** The legitimate power to make decisions, give binding orders, allocate company funds, and utilize organizational resources. **Authority flows downward**.
    2. **Responsibility (The Obligation to Perform):** The moral and contractual obligation of a subordinate to execute the assigned task conscientiously. **Responsibility flows upward**.
    3. **Accountability (The Answerability for Outcomes):** The final answerability of a person for the completion and outcome of the assigned task. A subordinate is accountable to their superior for their performance.
  - **Can Accountability Ever Be Delegated? (The Principle of Absolute Accountability):**
    - **NO, accountability can NEVER be delegated.**
    - While a manager can delegate authority and operational tasks, the manager remains **100% answerable** to higher top executives for the final outcome.
    - *Classic Example:* If a Chief Financial Officer delegates tax filing to an accountant, and the accountant misses the statutory deadline, the CFO cannot escape responsibility by blaming the clerk before the Board of Directors. The CFO remains absolutely accountable.
  - **Barriers to Effective Delegation:**
    - *On the Part of Superiors:* Egotistical belief that "I can do it better myself", fear of subordinate outshining them, reluctance to share prestige, and lack of trust in subordinates.
    - *On the Part of Subordinates:* Fear of criticism and making mistakes, lack of self-confidence and training, and inadequate incentives or rewards for extra responsibility.
* **Examiner Tip:** Explicitly emphasize that authority is delegated, but accountability remains absolute and cannot be delegated.
