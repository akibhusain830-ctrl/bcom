## 1. Motivation: Process & Theories

### Meaning & Financial vs. Non-Financial Motivators
- **Financial Motivators:** Basic salary, bonuses, performance-linked pay, stock options (ESOPs), profit sharing.
- **Non-Financial Motivators:** Public recognition, status, job enrichment, career advancement opportunities, job security.

### Core Theories of Motivation
1. **Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs:**
   - Physiological $\rightarrow$ Safety & Security $\rightarrow$ Social/Affiliation $\rightarrow$ Esteem $\rightarrow$ Self-Actualization.
   - Unsatisfied needs motivate; a satisfied need no longer serves as an active driver.
2. **McGregor’s Theory X and Theory Y:**
   - **Theory X:** Assumes people are lazy, avoid responsibility, and require coercive supervision.
   - **Theory Y:** Assumes work is natural, people are self-directed, creative, and seek responsibility.
3. **Herzberg’s Motivator-Hygiene Theory:**
   - **Hygiene Factors (Extrinsic):** Salary, working conditions, company policy. When absent, they cause dissatisfaction; when present, they only prevent dissatisfaction.
   - **Motivator Factors (Intrinsic):** Achievement, recognition, challenging work, growth. These actively generate high job satisfaction and motivation.

## 3. Communication: Process & Digital Revolution

### Elements of the Communication Process
```
[ Sender ] ──► [ Encoding ] ──► [ Message / Channel ] ──► [ Receiver ] ──► [ Decoding ]
    ▲                                                                           │
    └────────────────────── [ FEEDBACK CHANNEL ] ◄──────────────────────────────┘
                     (Noise / Semantic Barriers Interfere)
```

### Role of IT & Social Media in Business Communication
- **Real-Time Collaboration:** Tools like Slack, Microsoft Teams, and Zoom eliminate time-zone frictions.
- **Enterprise Social Networks:** Internal platforms (Yammer) facilitate bottom-up employee voice.
- **External Public Engagement:** Twitter/X, LinkedIn, and Instagram enable instant brand updates and direct customer feedback.
- **Challenges:** Information overload, data security vulnerabilities, and loss of confidential information.

## 4. Controlling & The Planning-Controlling Relationship

### The Controlling Process
1. Establishing Standards of Performance (KPIs, time, cost).
2. Measuring Actual Performance.
3. Comparing Actual Performance with Standards.
4. Analyzing Variances (Management by Exception & Critical Point Control).
5. Taking Corrective Actions.

### Interrelationship Between Planning and Controlling
- **Twin Sides of a Coin:** Planning establishes the targets; controlling assesses whether they were met.
- *"Planning without control is meaningless; controlling without planning is blind."*
- **Planning is Looking Ahead; Controlling is Looking Back.**

### Zero-Base Budgeting (ZBB)
- A modern budgeting technique where every program/expenditure begins with a **zero base**.
- No historical baseline is assumed; every departmental manager must justify every single rupee requested based on cost-benefit merit.
